StoveCam

Raspberry Pi 5RGB cameraAdafruit thermal PythonVision models
Lawrence Berkeley National Lab Full write-up ↗
What it isA Raspberry Pi 5 over the stove with a normal camera and an Adafruit thermal camera. It grabs a pair of photos every 60 s, and it's been running in three people's actual kitchens.
What I didMost of the hardware. I kept redoing the mounts and wiring until it worked reliably in every kitchen. I also co-wrote the Python pipeline that went through 40,000+ photo pairs: the Pi throws out dark frames, a small vision model checks if anyone's cooking, and a bigger one labels the food, method, cookware and burner.
Hardest partFalse positives. A burner that's empty but still hot from earlier looks just like cooking to the thermal camera, and that was most of our errors.
How I fixed itMake the model check the normal photo too, so if the burner's empty there it ignores the heat. Then we hand‑graded 506 of them to see how it did.

FPV drones

BetaflightArduPilotExpressLRS Storm32MAVLinkBlackbox logs
Personal builds Reel and gallery ↗
What it is8 drones so far, from 45 mm up to 5 in, and 400+ hours flying them. I have my FAA Part 107. I also worked on a 2‑axis brushless gimbal on a Storm32 controller that the flight controller talks to over UART/MAVLink.
What I didI pick the motors, ESCs and props, solder in the flight controller, receiver, GPS and video, calibrate the GPS, compass, barometer and IMU, and tune the PIDs from blackbox logs. And when one crashes, I fix it.
Hardest partAn ESC problem I had to track down.
How I fixed itWent over everything with a multimeter, found the tiny connections that had broken, and re-soldered them.

S.P.L.O.O.G.E

COLMAP3DGSRust WebGPUWebGLPython
3D reconstruction from drone footage Full write-up ↗
What it isI flew a drone over two spots six days apart and turned both flights into one photorealistic 3D scene. You can fly around it in the browser next to the real footage. I also wrote it up like a paper.
What I didThe whole pipeline. COLMAP figures out where the camera was, Brush (a Rust/WebGPU splat trainer I added a depth-distortion loss to) builds the scene, WebGL plays it back, and Python glues it together.
Hardest partThe ground had see-through holes in it when you flew low. I spent a week treating it like a training problem and measuring everything.
How I fixed itTurns out it was one setting in the renderer. The near plane was 0.2, so it clipped off the ground right under the camera, and no amount of retraining could fix pixels the renderer was throwing away. Changing it cut the holes 11.6×.
